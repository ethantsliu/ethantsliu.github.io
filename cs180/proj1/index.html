01

Background

Recovering color
from three exposures

The color is already in the scans!

I split each scan into three equal sections, ordered blue, green, and red. Since the exposures don’t line up exactly, I keep blue fixed and find shifts for green and red, then combine them in RGB order.

Cathedral separated into its three channels. View the original stacked plate ↗

Offsets are (x, y) in original-resolution pixels, relative to blue. Positive values move right and down.

02

Starting with the JPEGs

Single-scale
alignment

I decided to test shifts from −15 to +15 pixels along each axis using np.roll and select the lowest L2 distance to a reference, in this case blue.

When scoring, we need to avoid matching the scan borders, so I exclude min(height,width) // 10 pixels from each side.

L2 distance

L₂(Δx, Δy) = √∑ (Cshifted − B)²

C is green or red; B is blue.

03

Working with larger scans

Image pyramids

If we downsample/downsize the image, then it will make larger shifts much cheaper to find!

We start at 1/16 resolution. This makes things significantly cheaper; so a 160-pixel shift becomes just 10 pixels here! So for channels larger than 1,000 pixels along the shorter side, let’s do that.

Then, at each finer level, I double the estimated shift and search within 2 pixels plus minus of it, continuing to full resolution. Each level uses L2 with the same border exclusion.

  1. 1/16Find the initial shift±15 px
  2. 1/8Double & refine±2 px
  3. 1/4Double & refine±2 px
  4. 1/2Double & refine±2 px
  5. 1/1Refine at full size±2 px

04

Bells & whistles

Aligning with
edge features

Different filters change brightness, but often preserve edges.

The Emir’s blue robe looks different across channels, which can mislead pixel-based L2. I instead compare edge maps (the sum of absolute differences from each pixel’s upper and left neighbors). Then I apply the resulting shifts to the original channels to preserve their colors.

Edge featureE(y, x) = |I(y, x) − I(y−1, x)| + |I(y, x) − I(y, x−1)|
Raw-pixel L2Edge-based L2↔

Church and Emir

Pixel-based L2 misaligns the Church photo's red channel at 214,63, whereas when we use edges, we get (-4, 58). For Emir, when we use edges, it changes the red from (57, 103) to (40, 107), reducing color fringes. Differences in brightness between channels can lead to a wrong initial shift. Since we only search within ±2 pixels at each finer level, we might not be able to fix it.
